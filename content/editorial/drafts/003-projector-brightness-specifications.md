# Projector Brightness Specifications: What Can You Compare?

Compare projector brightness only after checking what the number measures and how it was obtained. Record the stated measurement method, whether the figure describes white or color output, and the operating mode. A larger number without that context is not enough to establish which projector suits your room.

## Read the label beside the number

Projector listings can describe different quantities using similar language. Use this table as a starting point for questions, rather than a conversion chart.

| Listing label | What to check before comparing |
|---|---|
| ANSI lumens | Find the manufacturer's specification and any measurement conditions. Keep the original label in your comparison. |
| Lumens measured under ISO 21118 | Record the standard reference, output type and mode. Do not invent a multiplier to convert it into another listing's number. |
| Color light output | Record separately from white light output and retain its measurement reference. |
| LED lumens | Request a documented projected-output measurement before treating the number as comparable to an ANSI or ISO-labelled figure. |
| Light-source lumens | Establish whether the claim concerns the light source rather than the projected image. |
| Lumens with no explanation | Ask for the original specification and method; mark the comparison unresolved. |

[BenQ's brightness explainer](https://www.benq.com/en-us/knowledge-center/knowledge/ansi-light-source-and-led-lumens-how-led-projectors-with-3000-lumens-are-a-marketing-ploy.html) distinguishes ANSI, LED and light-source claims. That distinction is useful when reading listings. For a purchase decision, request documented output for the exact model instead of applying a generic marketing conversion to an unknown product.

## ANSI versus ISO: keep the evidence attached

Treat these labels as references to how output is specified, not as currency units with an automatic exchange rate. This guide does not establish numerical equivalence between different measurement procedures. When two shortlisted models use different labels, retain each manufacturer's wording and ask for clarification of the measurement basis and settings.

A practical comparison row should include the exact model, regional version, published figure, method, output type, mode and source URL. If one of those details is missing, leave it marked as unknown. Filling gaps with assumptions makes a comparison look more precise than the evidence allows.

## White and color output answer different questions

[Epson's color-brightness guide](https://epson.com/projector-guide-how-to-buy-a-projector-color-brightness) identifies separate white and color brightness specifications and cites ISO 21118 for white output and IDMS 15.4 for color output. Keep these as separate fields: do not add them together into a total-lumen figure.

Nor should either field be treated as a complete measure of image quality. Use it alongside the requirements for your content and room, rather than turning a single specification into an overall product ranking.

## Check which mode the figure describes

The same projector can have different published output figures in different operating modes. For example, [Epson's Pro L1060-series specification page](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd5/cpd57486/source/specifications/reference/pl1060u_1075u/spex_general_pl1060u_1075u.html) lists different white-output values for Normal and Quiet or Extended modes. This is a documentation example, not a claim about the output of another model.

Record the mode you expect to use. If a supplier gives a brightness figure from one mode and a noise or power figure from another, do not present those numbers as a single demonstrated operating condition. Ask for the relevant combination or mark it as unverified.

## Connect the specification to your room

Before requesting a recommendation, write down the intended image size, screen, daytime or evening use, window lighting and whether blinds can be closed. Share those details along with the exact model names. Our [room-first buying guide](/en/guides/how-to-choose-a-projector-for-your-room) provides the broader planning checklist.

For a demonstration, request the intended image size and a room-lighting condition close to your own. Record the picture and power modes. This is a proposed comparison method, not a claim that Reach has demonstrated or measured the models.

Screen choice also belongs in that discussion. Use the [white, ALR and CLR screen guide](/en/guides/white-vs-alr-vs-clr-projector-screen) to identify compatibility questions rather than assuming that any screen makes a projector suitable for daylight viewing.

## Five questions to send the seller

1. What is the exact model and regional version?
2. Where is the original manufacturer brightness specification?
3. What method and output type does the figure describe?
4. Which operating mode and stated conditions apply?
5. What evidence is available for the screen size and room conditions I plan to use?

### Can I convert LED lumens into ANSI lumens with one multiplier?

Do not base your purchase on a universal multiplier for an unspecified product. Request a documented measurement for the model and preserve the original specification label.

### Does the largest lumen number mean the best picture?

The number alone does not establish overall picture quality or suitability. First verify that the figures describe comparable measurements, then assess the full installation and content requirements.

### Does this guide specify a minimum lumen number for daylight?

No. A recommendation needs the intended image size, screen and lighting conditions. A blanket threshold would leave out those decision factors.
