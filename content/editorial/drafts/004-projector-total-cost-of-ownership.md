# Projector Total Cost of Ownership: A Procurement Worksheet

Compare projector proposals over the same planning period and scope. Start with the complete installed system, then add estimated energy, maintenance and service costs. Record optional contingency separately so the buyer can see which costs are quoted, calculated or assumed.

The lowest projector-only price does not establish the lowest cost for an installed project. A useful comparison makes the missing items visible before an order is approved.

## Define one common comparison basis

Record the number of rooms, required image size, installation constraints, annual operating hours and planning period. Use one currency and record the date and basis of any currency conversion. State how taxes are treated and use the same convention for each proposal; the appropriate treatment should be confirmed by the buyer's finance team.

Compare proposals that meet the same functional requirements. A cheaper configuration that omits a required lens, control interface or installation service needs those omissions resolved before totals are meaningful. Start with our [business-buyer RFQ checklist](/en/guides/projector-rfq-checklist-for-business-buyers).

## Build the project budget by cost category

The worksheet below is a suggested structure. Blank amounts are unknown costs, not zero costs.

| Cost category | Items to include where applicable | Basis to record |
|---|---|---|
| Projection equipment | Projectors, required lenses and agreed accessories | Quantity × quoted unit price |
| Screen and mounting | Screen, mount, cabinet and mounting hardware | Itemized quote |
| Signal and control | Cables, adapters, switchers, audio and control equipment | Agreed system design and quote |
| Installation | Site work, access equipment, alignment, commissioning and training | Written scope and exclusions |
| Delivery | Freight and charges assigned to the buyer | Quote terms; avoid double-counting included charges |
| Energy | Active and standby electricity | Watts, hours and local tariff assumptions |
| Maintenance | Applicable cleaning, filters, consumables and labor | Exact manual and service quote |
| Service | Support agreement, access, removal, reinstallation and transport if excluded | Coverage and exclusions |
| Contingency | Agreed spare equipment or temporary replacement allowance | Buyer-defined scenario |
| End of use | Removal and disposal, less any documented residual-value assumption | Buyer policy and explicit estimate |

For delivery responsibilities, use the [DDP versus DAP guide](/en/guides/ddp-vs-dap-projector-shipping) alongside the actual quotation. This worksheet assigns no duty rate and assumes no tax-inclusive shipping promise.

## Calculate electricity from a stated operating scenario

For each operating state:

**Energy in kWh = power in watts ÷ 1,000 × hours in that state.**

Multiply the result by the electricity price per kWh and the number of units. Add the active and standby amounts. Use wall-input power consumption, not optical light-source output power. Where measured power is unavailable, label the manufacturer figure and selected mode as an estimate basis.

Here is an illustrative calculation using invented planning inputs, not a Reach quote or a tested projector:

- Four projectors, each assumed to draw 250 W while active.
- 1,500 active hours per projector per year.
- Assumed tariff: USD 0.20/kWh, held constant.

Annual active-use energy is 4 × 0.250 × 1,500 = **1,500 kWh**. Annual active-use electricity cost is 1,500 × USD 0.20 = **USD 300**. Over five years, that component is **USD 1,500** at the unchanged assumptions.

This example excludes standby, accessories, cooling, fixed utility charges and tariff changes. Add applicable costs separately. If you allocate a full year's hours across operating states, keep those hours mutually exclusive so active and standby time are not counted twice.

## Read light-source life and maintenance separately

A light-source-hour specification is not a complete cost or warranty statement. [Epson's LightScene light-source specifications](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd5/cpd55369/source/specifications/reference/lightscene_100_105/spex_laser_ls100_105.html) illustrate that life depends on mode, environment and usage, and that brightness decreases over time. Do not transfer a model's rating to another product or treat it as a guaranteed date when the whole projector will need replacement.

Check the exact maintenance instructions too. One [Epson laser-projector maintenance page](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd4/cpd40296/source/maintenance/concepts/maint_projector_laser.html) includes lens, filter and vent care despite a sealed light source. This shows why a laser label alone does not justify a zero-maintenance budget; requirements vary by model.

For each shortlisted unit, ask which tasks are permitted for the user, which require service, and what labor or access arrangements the site needs. Use current quotations for consumables and service rather than inventing a replacement schedule from an advertised maximum-life number.

## Make downtime assumptions visible

A classroom, meeting room and event venue can have different continuity requirements. Ask the project owner how long a room can be unavailable and whether a spare, rental or alternative room is acceptable.

If you include a spare unit as a purchased item, do not count that same purchase again in a contingency allowance. Record any further downtime allowance as a scenario, with its assumptions. Do not describe it as an expected failure cost without supporting reliability data.

## Compare totals without hiding uncertainty

Use this budget structure over the agreed period:

**Planning total = installed acquisition cost + energy + maintenance + service + selected contingency + end-of-use cost − stated residual value.**

Every amount should have a source, date, inclusion note and confidence label: quoted, calculated or provisional. Leave unpriced requirements visible. Run a second scenario for uncertain inputs such as annual hours or service scope instead of presenting one total as guaranteed.

This is an undiscounted planning worksheet. It does not calculate net present value, financing costs or tax benefits. Add those only when your finance team requires them and supplies the assumptions.

### Are laser projectors always cheaper to own?

This worksheet makes no universal cost claim. Compare the complete installation, usage and service requirements for the actual alternatives.

### Should all warranty-period repairs be budgeted at zero?

Check the written coverage and exclusions. Record who pays for access, removal, transport and reinstallation where relevant; do not infer those services from the word warranty.
