## Instagram carousel

### Working title

Will Your UST Projector Actually Fit the Cabinet?

### Slide copy

1. **Will your UST projector actually fit the cabinet?** Five measurements to take before you buy.
2. **Choose the screen size first.** The same projector sits in a different position for a 100-inch and a 120-inch image.
3. **Check two distances.** Measure from the screen surface to the projector reference point, then check the vertical offset to the bottom of the image.
4. **Measure the whole cabinet.** Width and depth are not enough. Include cabinet height, rear cable space and the projector's light path.
5. **Plan the audio and ventilation.** A soundbar, center speaker or enclosed shelf can interfere with the image or airflow.
6. **Do not solve furniture mistakes with keystone.** Digital correction is for final adjustment, not incorrect placement.
7. **Send us five details.** Projector model / screen model / target size / cabinet dimensions / front and side photos.

### Caption

A UST projector can create a large image from close to the wall—but a few centimetres can decide whether the image fits the screen, sits too high or pushes the cabinet into the room.

Before you buy, confirm:

1. Target screen size
2. Model-specific projection distance
3. Vertical image offset
4. Cabinet width, depth and height
5. Cable, ventilation and speaker clearance

Use the exact manufacturer drawing or calculator for the model. A generic “UST distance” is not enough.

## LinkedIn post

### Copy

Two projector quotations can list the same unit price and still produce very different landed costs.

Before comparing international offers, ask every supplier to quote against the same logistics brief:

• exact quantity and destination postcode;
• packed dimensions and gross weight per carton;
• total carton or pallet count;
• chargeable-weight rule and volumetric divisor;
• transport mode and estimated transit window;
• DAP or DDP followed by the named destination and “Incoterms 2020”;
• remote-area, unloading and address-correction exclusions.

Why it matters: couriers normally charge the greater of actual and volumetric weight. The destination postcode can also change route availability and remote-area charges. And DAP must never be presented as duty paid: under DAP, import clearance and duties remain for the buyer; under DDP, the seller takes responsibility for import clearance and applicable import duties and taxes.

A comparable RFQ is more useful than a lower-looking freight line.
